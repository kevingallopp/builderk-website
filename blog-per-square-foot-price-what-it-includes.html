What a Per Square Foot Price Really Includes

  • October 5, 2026
  • 4 min read
  • BuilderK Team
Get my free consultation Call (239) 230 4868
Ask three builders for a price per square foot and you'll get three different numbers, because they're not always measuring the same thing. One figure might cover structure only. Another might fold in site work nobody has priced yet. Before you compare numbers, you need to know what's actually inside them.

What a Planning Rate Is Telling You

A per square foot rate is a planning tool, not a quote. It gives you a realistic range to work with before plans exist, so you can size a project to a budget instead of designing first and getting surprised later.

BuilderK publishes Central Florida planning rates based in the Orlando metro: Standard runs $160 to $180 per square foot, Midrange runs $180 to $220, and Luxury runs $220 to $280 or more. The build level sets the structural systems, roof and exterior. Custom interiors are included at every level, meaning you choose your own cabinets, countertops, flooring and fixtures regardless of tier.

A formal proposal follows a plan and site review. Until that happens, the planning rate is your starting point, not your final number.

What Typically Sits Inside the Rate

  • The structural shell: foundation, walls, roof framing or block, and exterior finish appropriate to the build level.
  • Mechanical, electrical and plumbing rough work and finish installations.
  • Interior finishes you select: cabinets, countertops, flooring and fixtures.
  • Construction management: a licensed general contractor coordinating subcontractors and county inspections through the build.

What Sits Outside the Rate

This is where budgets go sideways if nobody spells it out early. The planning rates do not include:

  • Land. The rate is a cost to build, not a cost to own the lot you're building on.
  • Financing costs. Loan fees, interest during construction and closing costs are separate from the construction budget itself.
  • Permit and impact fees. These are billed at cost and vary by county and municipality.
  • Utility connections. Bringing power, water or sewer to the lot, or setting up well and septic, is priced based on what the site actually needs.
  • Unusual site work. Clearing, grading, retaining walls or difficult access can add cost depending on the lot.
  • Elevated foundations. Lots that require an elevated foundation carry added structural cost beyond standard slab construction.
  • Landscaping. What goes in the ground after construction is a separate budget line.

Coastal and flood zone projects typically cost more, since site conditions, foundation requirements and materials often shift to meet what the location demands.

Why Two Lots Can Price Differently at the Same Rate

Two homeowners can pick the same floor plan and the same build level and still land on different total budgets, because the lot does the deciding on everything outside the rate. A lot with utilities already at the street costs less to connect than one that needs a long utility run. A lot in a flood zone may need a different foundation approach than one on high, dry ground. None of that shows up in a per square foot number, because a per square foot number assumes a typical site, and no two sites are identical.

This is part of why a lot and budget review happens early, before plans are drawn. It's also worth checking a lot's access, utilities and flood zone status before you get attached to it. Our guide on what to check first when buying a Florida lot walks through what to look for.

How to Use the Calculator Without Misreading It

BuilderK's free cost calculator applies the published planning rates to the square footage and build level you enter, so you get a range to plan around. It is not a quote, and it cannot account for your specific lot's utilities, flood zone status or site conditions, because those details come later in a plan and site review.

Use it the way it's meant to be used: as a starting range to see whether a project size and finish level fit your budget before you invest in plans. If the range works, the next step is a real conversation about your lot and your numbers, not a guess based on square footage alone.

What This Means for Your Budget

A planning rate tells you what construction tends to cost per square foot in Central Florida. It does not tell you what your project will cost, because your project includes a specific lot with specific conditions, a specific loan structure and specific fees tied to your county. Treat the rate as a sizing tool, not a final budget, and build in room for the line items that sit outside it.
