Construction Loan Questions Florida Lot Owners Ask

  • November 11, 2026
  • 4 min read
  • BuilderK Team
Get my free consultation Call (239) 230 4868

If you own a lot in Florida and you're starting to look into financing, you probably have questions that feel basic but matter a lot. How does a construction loan actually work. What do lenders look at. Does owning the lot outright help you. This post answers the questions we hear most often from lot owners.

BuilderK is a licensed Florida general contractor, not a lender or mortgage broker. We introduce independent lenders who offer construction loans and we prepare the builder documents they request: plans, specifications and budget. The lender decides approval, rates, fees and terms. With that said, here's what lot owners usually want to know.

What do lenders commonly review?

Every lender and program is different, but construction loan applications commonly involve a review of credit, income, debt, reserves, down payment, the appraisal and overall project feasibility. That last one, project feasibility, is where your builder documents matter: the plans, specifications and budget the lender uses to understand what's being built and whether the numbers line up.

Because requirements vary by lender and program, two borrowers with similar finances can see different outcomes depending on which lender they work with. That's part of why BuilderK introduces more than one option rather than assuming one lender fits every situation.

What is a draw, exactly?

A construction loan doesn't hand you the full loan amount on day one. Instead, funds release in stages called draws, tied to milestones in the build. As the foundation is poured, the walls go up, the roof goes on and so on, the lender releases the next portion of funds to keep the project moving.

This is normal and it protects everyone involved: the lender isn't releasing money faster than the work is happening, and you're not paying interest on funds that haven't been used yet. If you want a sense of how draws line up with the actual build schedule, our realistic Florida build timeline walks through the phases a draw schedule typically follows.

I already own my lot. Does that help?

It can. If you own your lot, its appraised equity may reduce or replace the cash down payment a lender requires. Whether that happens, and by how much, depends on the appraisal, the total project cost, your qualifications as a borrower and the specific loan program.

The appraisal is what tells the lender what the lot is actually worth today, not what you paid for it or what you think it's worth. If you haven't had your lot appraised yet or you're still evaluating a lot you're considering, our post on what to check first when buying a Florida lot covers some of the groundwork that comes before financing conversations even start.

Single close or two closings, which is better?

Depending on the program, a single close loan combines construction financing and the permanent mortgage into one closing. Two separate closings, one for construction and one to refinance into a permanent loan once the home is done, is the other path, and it may offer different options.

Neither is automatically the better choice. It depends on the loan programs available to you, your long term plans and what the actual numbers look like side by side. The only reliable way to compare is to look at the Loan Estimates for each option once they're in front of you. We go deeper on how single close loans work in this explainer for lot owners.

Does a construction to permanent loan convert automatically?

No. With an approved construction to permanent loan, the conversion to the permanent mortgage follows the lender's terms and completion conditions. It's not automatic just because construction wrapped up. There are conditions tied to the loan that need to be met first, and those conditions come from the lender, not from the builder.

This is one of the reasons the builder documents matter so much throughout the build. Keeping plans, specifications and budget consistent with what the lender approved helps avoid surprises when it's time for that conversion.
